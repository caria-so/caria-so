Facebook Reels, end of a long day. Not proud of the source. But that's where I found this an interview with Federico Faggin. What stayed with me wasn't his theory of consciousness. It was one throwaway detail: a researcher can run **two research programs at once**. One visible, it is the work everyone knows you for. One private, years of it, often nothing to do with your job, but quietly rewriting how you think about everything else. Turns out the man gave hours a day to the second one, kept it walled off from his professional life and even from his wife. Well, I've been trying to do the same thing for years, let me tell you, I can confirm the hard part is not the research. It's the wife.

## Who is Faggin

Intel's first commercial microprocessor. Founder of Synaptics. The first neural networks. A mountain of patents. He then devoted himself to the problem of consciousness: books, interviews, ingenious theories to answer questions that traditional computer science files under *not our problem*. Whether he's right or wrong is beside the point.

## What the interview covers

Must say the interview snippet itself isn't great until the end. At one point, he mentions personal *experiences* that drove him to consciousness research where he found just superficial answers. Doesn't say more about the nature of those experiences, but I bet it was an OBE-like. Ultimately, he states that his scientific career has developed in parallel with independent, unfunded, unpublished research. This is fascinating.

## The claim

**Research is not exhausted by your discipline.** Not even close.

Everyone I take seriously is running something underneath. Slower. Philosophical, contemplative, spiritual, pick your word. No funding, no peer review, no CV line. And it's the thread that motivates everything downstream. Two directions:

- **Outward** — problems in the world. Runs on the cognitive system.
- **Inward** — the self. Runs on the cardiocirculatory system.

Nobody talks about the second because it's hard to do, impossible to formalise, and unmeasurable in any way a hiring committee recognises. Hours a day, for years, no guarantee of anything, plenty of frustration. And then one day the visible work starts drawing from it. You can't separate Faggin the engineer from Faggin the man running the other program. Agree with his conclusions or not — without the second one, the first doesn't make sense.
